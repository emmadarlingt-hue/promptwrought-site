Labels & marks

The small typographic furniture. All of it is Courier Prime, uppercase, widely tracked — and all of it is structural. A label says what part of the document you are in; it never editorialises.

Outline is the default. Solid ink marks the issue number, and is also the selected state of a filter. The quiet variant marks an entry that hasn’t been struck yet. No tag is ever filled with hot metal — that would put two accents on one page.

Categories are links, not buttons — <a href="/lexicon/asking"> inside a labelled <nav> — so a filtered view is shareable and crawlable. The selected one carries aria-current="page", which is what draws the solid fill; there is no separate modifier class for it.

Edges. A chip’s border is --control-edge (ink at 55%, 3.5 : 1), not the --rule used for dividers — at 28% a rule measures 1.8 : 1, which is fine for a line between paragraphs and not fine for the edge of something you can click. On ink the chip flips to --hot-metal-light text with --control-edge-reversed; --hot-metal-deep must never appear on ink.

The interpunctthe single most important mark in the system

prompt · wrought

A middle dot in --hot-metal, flanked by thin spaces (&thinsp;), marking the syllable break in a headword. It is the brand’s one flash of colour above the fold and the anchor of the monogram. Set it in the display face at the same size as the surrounding word — never scaled, never bolded, never a bullet.
