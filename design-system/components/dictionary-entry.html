The signature block. Every issue opens with one, and it is the reason the whole system is built from rules and baselines rather than cards — it has to look like something out of a dictionary, because it is one.

The word this is named for

prompt · wrought

\ ˈprɒm(p)t-ˌrɔːt \ adj.
  1. 1.

    Made by asking. Shaped in language rather than in metal, wood or stone.

  2. 2.

    Of a made thing whose maker cannot fully say which parts are theirs.

  3. 3.

    A lexicon, published in issues. One coined word each time, with its definition, its roots, and the case for keeping it.

Anatomyfive parts, this order, no exceptions

1 · Eyebrow
Utility label in hot-metal-deep. Says what the block is. Sentence case, no full stop.
2 · Headword
Display XL, condensed, lowercase, syllable-broken with the hot-metal interpunct and thin spaces. This is the only h1 on the page.
3 · Gloss line
Pronunciation in slashes (iron) and part of speech (hot metal, italic), baseline-aligned with a 1.1em gap, wrapping to a second line on narrow screens.
4 · Senses
Numbered list on a 2.25rem gutter. Utility numerals in hot metal, definitions in body at 1.06em. Two to four senses; the last one is always the meta-sense — what the publication itself is.
5 · Restraint
No rule above, no rule below, no background. The white space around it is the frame.

Pronunciation uses backslash-space delimiters (\ … \) in the Merriam-Webster house style, not IPA slashes, and is set in the display face so the diacritics render properly. Part of speech is abbreviated and italicised — n., v., adj. — and is the one place hot metal appears in the gloss.
