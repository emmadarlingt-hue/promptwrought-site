Surfaces & footer

Four surfaces, stacked. A page is built by choosing an order for them — there is no other layout decision to make.

The band stackhairline between consecutive parchment bands

Parchment — the default

Where everything is read.

Entries, prose, process, archive. Most of a page is this surface, and consecutive bands are separated by a 1px hairline at 14% — not by a colour change.

Parchment deep — the aside

One shade down, for the editor’s note or a standfirst. No hairline needed; the tone change is the divider.

Hot metal deep — the ask

One per page, and only for subscribing.

The single filled accent band. If a page has two of these, one of them is not really an ask.

Ink — the close

Ends the page. Reversed wordmark, one line of utility metadata.

Footer

Promptwrought

Est. MMXXVI · London

© 2026 Promptwrought

Wordmark reversed at 90% opacity on the left, the founding line and the copyright on the right, wrapping to stacked on narrow screens. The year is set in roman numerals in the founding line so 2026 does not read twice; the copyright keeps arabic, which is the convention people scan for. Nothing else goes in the footer — no nav, no social row, no newsletter form repeated. On ink, links are parchment and underline on hover; labels sit at 70% opacity.

Reversed systemtoken swaps when the surface is ink

Text becomes --parchment. Secondary text becomes parchment at 70%, or --parchment-dim (#C9BFA6) — the one place that token is used.
Rules become --rule-light: parchment at 30%.
The accent steps up, not down. On ink, accent text is --hot-metal-light (#E0703F) at 5.1 : 1. --hot-metal (#C2451C) reaches only 3.3 : 1 on ink, so on dark it is limited to marks and display type — the monogram interpunct, a large numeral. --hot-metal-deep must never appear on ink at all (2.5 : 1); it is a light-surface token only.
Never butt a hot-metal band directly against an ink band — the two darks fight and the join reads as a rendering error. Put a parchment band between them.
