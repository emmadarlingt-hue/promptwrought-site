From the desk

The -wright suffix is one of the oldest job descriptions in English. It comes from Old English wyrhta, a worker or maker, and it survives now almost entirely in trades that have gone: the wheelwright, the shipwright, the millwright, the cartwright. Only the playwright is still at work.

Which is exactly what makes it available. The suffix is unclaimed. And wrought — the old past participle of work — carries something that made does not: the sense of a thing worked into shape by hand, with effort, and with the marks of the working still on it.

Emma Darling, editor
