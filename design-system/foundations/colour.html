Colour

Two surfaces and one accent. Parchment and ink do all the structural work; hot metal appears once per screen, never twice. If a page needs a second accent, the page is doing too much.

Parchment dim #C9BFA6

Hot metal #C2451C
--hot-metal

The accent, on light — but only at 24px and up: the interpunct in a headword, rules, and display type. Nothing smaller.

Hot metal deep #A63914
--hot-metal-deep

The same accent at text sizes on light: links, labels, and any band filled behind parchment copy.

Hot metal light #E0703F
--hot-metal-light

The accent on ink only. Never on parchment — it drops to 2.5 : 1 there.

The accent has three values because one value cannot do the job. Reds sit in an awkward part of the luminance range: a single red is either too dark to read on ink or too light to read on parchment. Deep (#A63914) on light · bright (#C2451C) for rules, marks and display type of 24px or more, at either end · light (#E0703F) on dark. They are close enough that no reader will notice the swap; the contrast checker will.

The trap in the current site. #C2451C behind parchment text measures 3.9 : 1 — below AA at body size, which is what the subscribe band does today with its 0.95em note. Filling that band with #A63914 instead takes it to 5.1 : 1 and looks all but identical.

Rule weightsstructure, never ornament

Masthead rule — 4px over 1px, ink

Major rule — 2px ink. Opens an entry.

Rule — 1px at 28%. Divides peers.

Hairline — 1px at 14%. Separates bands.

Control edge — 1px at 55%. Bounds anything clickable.

Dividers and control edges are different jobs. A rule between paragraphs at 28% measures 1.8 : 1 and that is fine — it is decoration for the eye, not information. The border of a chip, an input, or an outlined button is the only thing telling you where the control ends, so it needs 3 : 1 (WCAG 1.4.11): --control-edge, ink at 55%, 3.5 : 1 on light, and --control-edge-reversed, parchment at 55%, 4.3 : 1 on ink.
